# SecureNet design direction

SecureNet should feel like a calm, capable engineering studio for businesses that need a real person to solve a technical problem. The user selected a corporate, trustworthy tone and asked for a less generic site.

- **Visual language:** editorial field notes, warm paper base, ink-blue structure, copper as a single action color.
- **Typography:** a readable humanist sans for body copy and a measured serif for the main promise. This separates practical service details from the company voice.
- **Layout:** one strong decision per section. Start with the inquiry path, then show what SecureNet does, then the Snap Cap case study as the single verifiable example.
- **Cards:** restrained, square-leaning panels used only where services or evidence need separation.
- **Motion:** hover and focus feedback only. No loops or ornamental entrances.
- **Dials:** ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 3 / MOTION 1.

## Decision reasons

- Ink-blue gives the site the restraint expected of a technical service, while copper reserves attention for contact actions.
- The paper-like background gives long service descriptions warmth without relying on gradients, glows, or glass.
- The editorial composition prioritizes a business problem and a clear path to contact instead of filling the page with generic feature blocks.
- The case study keeps one real external destination, Snap Cap Clothing, and removes unverified metrics.
