# SecureNet design direction

SecureNet should feel like a capable engineering studio for businesses that need a real person to solve a technical problem. The user selected a corporate, trustworthy tone and provided a bright modular editorial reference with bold abstract geometry.

- **Visual language:** bright modular editorial pages, a soft cool-grey canvas, white panels, and flat cobalt, orange, and green wayfinding shapes.
- **Typography:** a geometric sans with oversized, close-set display type. It makes the service promise direct while keeping technical details easy to scan.
- **Layout:** one strong decision per section. Start with the inquiry path, then show what SecureNet does, then the Snap Cap case study as the single verifiable example.
- **Cards:** large rounded white panels used only where services or evidence need separation. The panels echo the supplied reference without copying its layouts.
- **Motion:** hover and focus feedback only. No loops or ornamental entrances.
- **Dials:** ENERGY 3 / RHYTHM 3 / MOTION 1.

## Decision reasons

- Cobalt, orange, and green distinguish contact, process, and service wayfinding while the cool-grey canvas keeps the site professional.
- Flat abstract shapes add an ownable visual motif for systems connecting people, software, and workplace support, without introducing fictional imagery.
- White panels create usable reading surfaces and reserve shadows for separation from the canvas only.
- The editorial composition prioritizes a business problem and a clear path to contact instead of filling the page with generic feature blocks.
- The case study keeps one real external destination, Snap Cap Clothing, and removes unverified metrics.
